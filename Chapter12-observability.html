Why observability matters
Netflix, Uber, and Google run distributed tracing across every service boundary — because at scale, the question isn't if something breaks, it's which of 200 services broke. Observability is the only way to answer that in minutes instead of hours.

Define an SLO, then alert on burn rate
Set a target like "99.9% of requests under 300ms," track the error budget, and alert only when you're burning it too fast. Everything else belongs on a dashboard, not a page.

Key Takeaways
What every engineer should know about observability.
🧵
Trace ID & Spans
A Trace ID follows a request everywhere; each service adds a span recording its own duration. Together they form a waterfall of the entire request lifecycle.
📊
P50 / P95 / P99
Never trust the average alone. Percentiles reveal the real experience of your slowest users — and that's who churns first.
🚨
SLOs & Error Budgets
Define what "good" means numerically, then alert only when you're burning your error budget too fast. Fewer, more meaningful pages.
🔍
Instrument Early
Add tracing, structured logs, and metrics before you scale. Retrofitting observability into 50 services under fire is far harder than building it in from service #1.
💡
The rule of thumb
Observability isn't optional at scale — it's how you find a needle in an increasingly large haystack. Trace every request, measure percentiles instead of averages, and alert on user impact instead of noise. You can't fix what you can't see.
